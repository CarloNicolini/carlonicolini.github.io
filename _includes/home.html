I work on machine learning and statistical physics, with a background in computational neuroscience. I’m interested in how complex systems organize information, from networks of brain regions to the internal representations of language models.

I’m currently a Senior AI Research Scientist at Ipazia and a core maintainer of skfolio, a Python library for portfolio optimization. This is where I collect my papers, software, and research notes.

My background

Computational neuroscience

Ph.D. research and postdoctoral work at IIT

Much of my early research took place at the Istituto Italiano di Tecnologia in Rovereto. During my Ph.D. in Applied Physics at the University of Verona, and later as a postdoctoral researcher at IIT, I studied the organization of brain functional connectivity networks.

I worked on community detection and its resolution limits, then on spectral entropy as a way to study network structure at different scales. That work brought together neuroscience, graph theory, and statistical mechanics. It still informs how I approach questions about representations and information in AI.

What I work on

Language models

I study how language models encode knowledge and how we can inspect their representations. My recent work also looks at planning, reasoning, and the evaluation of AI systems.

Statistical physics & networks

Entropy and statistical mechanics provide much of the language I use to think about complex systems. I work on network models, spectral methods, and information theory.

Portfolio optimization

Through skfolio, I work on optimization and risk modeling in Python. I’m particularly interested in how portfolio methods are estimated, compared, and tested on data they have not seen.
